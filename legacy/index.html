    Questions

    Everything people ask before they start

    Short answers, no small print.

    What exactly is a salary timer?
    A salary timer is a live counter that converts your working time into money as it passes. Instead of calculating what you earned at the end of a month, it shows the number moving in real time — so you can see the value of an hour, a meeting, or an extra shift while it's happening.
    How does it calculate my salary per second?
    It takes your pay, converts it to an hourly rate using your real schedule (paid hours per day × working days × 52), then divides by 3,600. That per-second figure is added only while you're inside a paid window of your shift — never during breaks, before you start, or after you clock out (unless you've turned on overtime).
    How do I work out my hourly rate from an annual salary?
    The common shortcut is salary ÷ 2,080, which assumes 40 hours a week, 52 weeks a year. It's wrong for almost everyone. This site does the honest version: your actual paid hours per day, minus unpaid breaks, times the number of days you actually work, times 52. Work a 4-day week and your hourly rate jumps; work 50-hour weeks and it drops.
    Does it count my lunch break as paid time?
    No — and that's deliberate. Any break you add is treated as unpaid, and the counter freezes while you're on it. If your lunch is genuinely paid, just don't add it as a break and the timer will keep running through it.
    Can I use it if I'm paid hourly instead of a salary?
    Yes, and it's the most accurate mode. Switch the pay type to hourly and the timer uses your rate directly — no annual-hours estimation involved. It's ideal for retail, hospitality, warehouse, agency and gig work.
    Does it handle overtime and shift work?
    Yes. Turn on overtime and the timer keeps counting after your shift ends at 1.25×, 1.5× or 2×, whichever matches your contract. Shifts that cross midnight are supported, so night shifts work correctly. Rotating patterns are handled by changing your working days.
    Is my salary data private?
    Completely. Everything is calculated in your browser and saved to your own device's local storage. There's no account, no server storing your numbers, no analytics on what you earn. Privacy mode blurs the figures on screen when someone's looking over your shoulder.
    Do I need to install an app or create an account?
    No. It's a website — open it, use it, close the tab. Your settings stay saved on that device for next time. It works on phones, tablets and desktops, and it keeps working offline once loaded.
    Is a salary timer accurate enough to rely on?
    For tracking and motivation, yes. It's a gross-pay estimate: it doesn't model tax, pension contributions, deductions or pay-period rounding, so treat it as "what I earned before deductions" rather than "what will hit my bank". Many people find the pre-tax number more motivating anyway.
    Can freelancers use it for billing?
    Yes — set an hourly rate and use the timer as a live session tracker. It won't produce invoices, but it will show you what a task is really worth, including the unpaid admin around it. Many freelancers use it to sanity-check quotes before sending them.
    Why does watching money tick up actually help?
    Because abstract rewards don't motivate behaviour; visible ones do. A salary arrives once a month, far from the effort that produced it. A counter that moves every second shortens that gap — which is why people report leaving on time more often, saying no to unpaid extras, and negotiating harder after a week of watching.
